The problem

Why the usual fixes don't stop the overspend

If that sounds like your last board meeting, you're in good company. The money is going out the door. The kit is getting older. And nobody can say with confidence whether this year's spend is going on the right things.

Most owners try to fix this one piece at a time. A new maintenance system here, a condition survey there, someone brought in to look at capital planning. Each one makes sense on its own. The overspend carries on anyway.

In my experience the problem sits in the joins between the pieces. The maintenance contractor is paid to fix things, so nobody is paid to say which things to stop fixing. The capital team plans replacements from a condition survey that's three years old. Finance sees the invoices but not the risk behind them. Every team does its job well, and the whole thing still leaks money.

That's what I mean by the systems approach. I look at your assets, your people, your contracts and your money as one connected system, and I start where the joins are, because that's where most of the waste and the nasty surprises come from.
